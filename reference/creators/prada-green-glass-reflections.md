## Why it works

- A **product-accuracy block** that lists every physical part (glass, cap, neck, relief) before any style talk.
- **One colour world** named in four tones (forest green, emerald, dark olive, near-black) matching the glass.
- **A lighting vocabulary built for glass:** moving highlights, narrow light sweeps, light *through* the glass,
  rim light for the edges. Glass reads as real when the light moves across and through it.
- **Product turns one way, camera orbits the other:** cheap, strong parallax.
- A fixed scene grammar: reveal from darkness → texture macro → front orbit → three-quarter → cap macro →
  silhouette → montage → hero.

## Adopt / Avoid (for our brands)

- **Adopt:** the glass lighting vocabulary; the turntable-versus-opposite-orbit move; the reveal-from-darkness opener;
  the scene grammar for the first half of a perfume film; naming the studio gradient in the product's own colours.
- **Avoid:** asking a video model to render brand text ("DAL 1913 must stay written exactly"). We never ask for
  words; supers and end cards go on in post. Avoid "morph" even in a negative (our linter fails it). Music direction
  is moot for our visuals-only deliverables. Use timecodes start-first, one camera move per shot.
- Used for: `ads/jpg-spec/ultra-male-12s/` (first six shots follow this grammar in navy glass).
