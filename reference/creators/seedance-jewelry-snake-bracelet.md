## Why it works

- The whole film is macro on clean black: nothing but the piece to render, so nothing else can go wrong.
- A subject lock that says where the stones are and where they are not ("no added diamonds beyond…").
- One light rising from black is the only event in most shots: slow, real, and it makes the sparkle.
- Longer shots (1.4-3.2 s) than our fashion cuts: jewellery needs time for the eye to land.
- A held last second for the end card.

## Adopt / avoid

- Adopt: black set, single rising light, "sharp starburst sparkle", slow controlled motion, quiet SFX, the stone-
  placement lock, a held ending, a multi-view reference.
- Adopt with care: "light sweeps up" worked on gems; on a flat print it came back as a glow flash (Avrana, 11 Nov).
- Avoid: "CRITICAL" caps and "ENTIRE FRAME" shouting (our lint flags hype; the sheet warning in plain words is
  enough), "zero film grain" (we add grain in the finish anyway), a "flash transition" (we keep hard cuts).
- Ours adds what theirs lacks: a worn shot on skin and one real physical event (petals), so the shop owner sees a
  person wearing it, not only a turntable.
