# 04 Director review: avrana / death-glory-12s
## Verdict: REVISE

## Notes (numbered, each: shot #, what changes, why)
1. **Run plan (story Hook line; storyboard "special handling" bullet 1; shots 1 and 4).** The 12 s run uses reference images only: Image 1 = ref 01, Image 2 = ref 02, Image 3 = ref 04. It has no first frame anywhere. Delete "Use ref 05 … as first frame for 1" and "First-frame fallback: ref 05". Write shot 1 in words inside the run. Why: Seedance takes `--ref` or `--first-frame`, never both in one request (rulebook §2). A first frame can only be a separate 4 s insert, cut in after the run.
2. **Fallbacks for shots 1 and 4: ref 05 is not a usable first frame.** As it stands, ref 05 is concrete in hard sun with a skateboard and a cup. A first frame is rendered exactly as given, so "ignore the skateboard and cup" only works for a reference image. The photo is also shot at about 45°, not top-down. It is square, so `--aspect adaptive` would return a 1:1 clip. The hoodie already lies flat in it, so no drop can happen. Plan instead one prepared 9:16 still: the hoodie face-down on charcoal-grey ash, seen top-down in flat overcast light, with the skull from ref 02 under a thin veil of ash and its outline faint. Ref 05 serves only as a guide to how the fabric folds. Make the same still with the hoodie removed as an empty ash plate.
   - Shot 1 insert: first frame is the ash plate, last frame is the prepared still, so the drop survives.
   - Shot 4 insert: first frame is the prepared still, and the gust clears the veil.
   - Each insert is its own request: 4 s, no `--ref`, `--aspect adaptive`.
3. **Shot 1 (story Hook and beat 1; storyboard row 1): the hoodie lands face-down with the skull up.** The ash ring bursts out, then falls back as a thin veil over the print. Cut as it settles. Why:
   - As boarded, it lands chest-up, but shot 4 shows it face-down in the same ash 2 s later with nothing causing the flip. That is a continuity break.
   - A top-down shot of the whole garment in 9:16 makes the "Avrana" script about 120 px wide at 720p. Seedance garbles text at that size, and this is the one shot we can't cut.
   - The skull on ash is the design's meaning and the stronger graphic.
   - The veil sets up the reveal in shot 4. The chest print still gets its moment in shot 7.
4. **Shot 2: macro on the tip of one printed antler on the lying back, not the chest filigree.** Grey flakes settle on it. The camera drifts right, as boarded. Change the product column to "Back, about 5%". Why: it follows from note 3, it removes the last risk of letters in a macro, and the cut into shot 3 becomes a match from the printed antler to the real bleached antler.
5. **The lying hoodie (shots 1, 2 and 4).**
   - Shot 4: change "low macro across the back" to a high-angle close looking almost straight down on the back, skull about 70% of the frame. Fix "back-down lying view" to "face-down, back up". "Back-down" would hide the skull. A grazing angle on a lying garment is where Seedance's upright bias takes over, and the foreshortening makes it invent antler points. A top-down view matches Image 2's flat view directly.
   - All three shots: board the hoodie as "empty, lying flat on the ash, no body inside, hood folded flat". Images 1 and 2 are ghost-mannequin photos with a hollow body shape. Without this wording, the model inflates the lying hoodie or stands it up. Use Images 1 and 2 for print, colour and cut only, not for pose or volume. Trim any upright frames in the edit.
6. **Shot 7: frame the top of the shot at the collarbone.** The figure walks toward the camera, so the chest print grows and the head stays out of the top. Change the camera move from "pulls back slowly" to "pushes in slowly". Hood up, for continuity with shot 5. Delete "Headless figure". Why: a pull-back widens the frame and shows the head, which is a face leak. "Headless" in a prompt can render a body with no head. Keep the POV swap as the backup.
7. **Shot 8: move it from the night rooftop to the same burnt ground, under the same low grey overcast but darker.** Embers rise from smouldering ground in the out-of-focus foreground and light the skull warm from low at the side. The figure stands about 3 m back. Update the continuity note: no night anywhere. Why: a night city rooftop breaks the ash world and the time of day between shots 7 and 9, and a black hoodie against a black sky loses its silhouette. The embers stay the one warm accent and still never touch the garment.
8. **Refs line (story and storyboard): add "Image 3 (ref 04): use only for how the hoodie hangs worn from behind, hood up; ignore the peach background and the flowers."** Why: otherwise the flowers and the warm peach get pasted into the ash world (rulebook §4, reference pasted in).

## What makes this ours (one paragraph)
The film acts out the design's own sentence: "From what remains, something extraordinary emerges." The hoodie falls into ash and is buried. The wind uncovers the antlered skull. A faceless wearer pulls the hood up, walks out of the smoke and climbs out of the ruin to stand on the ridge. The chain from printed antler to real antler to skull, the gust clearing ash off this exact print, and a figure that ash can't stick to all come from this one design. No other brand could run the film. One cold world of ash, black and bone white, with one warm ember accent, no faces and no words from the model.
