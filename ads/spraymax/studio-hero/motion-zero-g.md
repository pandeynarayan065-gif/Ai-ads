High-end product commercial, filmed for real on a studio set with a motion-control camera, in extreme slow motion. It continues exactly from the first frame: the same Spraymax bottle standing on the same wet slate slab on top of the same glowing Himalayan pink salt block, against the same dark charcoal backdrop, with the same lighting.

The bottle stays perfectly still and upright and never changes shape or size, and its printed sticker label stays exactly as in the first frame, sharp in every frame.

Gravity switches off. The splashing water around the base stops falling and hangs in the air. Then hundreds of drops of clear water and small, glowing chunks of pink Himalayan salt crystal lift gently off the wet slate and the salt block and float upward around the bottle. They drift up in slow spirals and form a slow-turning ring around the bottle at the height of its label. Some drops are as small as grains of sand, others as big as marbles. Each round drop is a perfect sphere of clear water that wobbles softly, catches the rim light as a bright sparkle, and shows a tiny upside-down reflection of the glowing salt. The salt crystals turn slowly and glow pink-orange from within. Fine mist and salt dust hang in the air like stars. Nothing touches the bottle.

Camera: one smooth, continuous move. A slow push-in toward the bottle with a gentle rise, ending on the upper half of the bottle with the "Spraymax" wordmark large in frame. Floating drops pass in front of the lens with real parallax and soft bokeh. Steady and precise: no shake, no cuts.

Light: the same soft key light from the front left and crisp rim light from behind on the right. The salt glows warm pink-orange. Deep blacks, rich contrast, true colours, crystal-clear focus on the bottle.

Sound: the splash cuts to near silence, then a soft, deep hum and faint glassy chimes as the drops drift and touch; no music, no voices. No people, no hands, no text or logos other than the bottle's own label.
