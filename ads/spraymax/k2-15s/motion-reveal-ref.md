Real documentary footage filmed by a mountaineer at about 8,000 metres on K2: a close-up, camera 40 cm away looking down at the snow. In a gap between dark, frost-covered granite rocks, a white spray bottle lies on its side, half buried in powder snow, only the cap and part of the bottle showing. A climber's gloved hand (a thick black-and-red down mitt crusted with frost) sweeps across it twice, brushing the snow away to reveal the bottle: it is exactly the Spraymax bottle from the reference images, a slim white bottle with a white ribbed collar and spray pump, a cloudy clear cap, and a white label with the indigo-blue "Spraymax" wordmark running along the length of the bottle, the small line "Instant Hair Volume & Texture" and a dark navy ocean-wave band at the base end. After the last sweep the hand pulls away and the bottle lies still, label facing up toward the camera, fully visible, letters sharp and correct. Loose powder slides off it and glitters in the low sun. Natural cold high-altitude daylight, low sun from the left, deep blue shadows, true colour, no golden tint. Handheld camera, almost still. Sound: wind and the crunch of snow, no music, no voices. No faces, no text other than the bottle's own label.
