Real expedition footage on the summit ridge of K2, filmed handheld on a phone just behind and slightly below the mountaineer's right shoulder. It continues exactly from the first frame: the same man, hair, red down suit, bottle, light and view. We only see the back of his head, his ear and his hair; his face stays turned away toward the view the whole time.

He is spraying his own hair. His raised right arm, in a thin black fleece glove, holds the Spraymax bottle upright beside the back of his head, about a hand's width from his hair, with the front label toward the camera. The bottle stays the same size and shape, and its printed label stays exactly as in the first frame.

At about one second he presses the pump once with his gloved index finger. The white pump head dips a few millimetres and springs back up. One short, sharp burst of fine mist shoots out of the nozzle and lasts less than half a second: a narrow white cone, dense at the nozzle tip, that widens as it travels and hits his hair a hand's width away. Backlit by the sun, the mist glows white, with tiny sparkling droplets at its edges, and where it lands, tiny droplets glint on the strands. The wind tears the rest of the mist away within a second. At about three seconds he lifts the bottle a little higher and presses again: a second, identical burst into the hair at his crown. Then he holds the bottle still, label to the camera, while the wind whips his hair.

Behind him are the knife-edge snow ridge, the sea of cloud and the jagged Karakoram peaks, with spindrift streaming off the ridge. Natural cold daylight, true colour, no golden tint. Real-time speed, slight handheld shake, no slow motion, no cinematic effects. Sound: roaring summit wind and two short, crisp hisses from the pump spray; no music, no voices. No faces, no text except the bottle's own label.
