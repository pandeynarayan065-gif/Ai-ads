Real expedition footage near the summit of K2, about 8,000 metres up, filmed handheld from behind and slightly to one side of a mountaineer, close, at shoulder height. He has just pulled off his climbing helmet and holds it against his chest with both bare, frost-cracked, scraped hands; the white helmet is scuffed and stained from the climb. Glacier goggles hang around his neck on their strap; harness straps and a carabiner sit over a worn red down suit.

His thick, dark, wild hair, flattened a moment ago by the helmet, is caught by a hard gust: it lifts into messy, textured volume, strands separating, and holds its shape with a dry matte look, not wet and not glossy. Spindrift streams past him; beyond him a sea of cloud and the jagged Karakoram peaks under a dark-blue sky.

He looks away toward the horizon the entire time. We only see the back and side of his head, his ear and his hair, never his face: no eyes, nose or mouth in any frame. He never turns toward the camera.

Low, hard sun from the side rims his hair with light. Natural cold daylight, true colour, no golden or warm tint, bright snow close to clipping. Real hair with individual strands and flyaways. Slight handheld movement, real-time speed, no slow motion, no cinematic effects. Sound: roaring wind and the creak of the down suit, no music, no voices. No faces, no text, no logos.
