# Bottle drop into water: the physics the prompt is built on

Real event: a 100 ml plastic bottle (density close to water, small air headspace) dropped ~40 cm,
upright, base first -> impact speed about 2.8 m/s. Filmed at 240 fps -> ~10x slow motion, so the
8 s clip covers ~0.8 s of real time: impact, plunge, cavity pinch-off, deceleration, buoyant stop.

| Phase | Physics | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | A flat (disk) nose traps a thin gas layer on its face; its escape drives the splash sheet and spray. Disk splash is very sensitive to tilt (<1 deg makes it asymmetric). | Belden et al. 2023, "Water entry of cups and disks", J. Fluid Mech. 963 A14 |
| Air cavity | Above a threshold speed of a few m/s an air cavity forms behind the body; the threshold depends on surface wettability (water-repellent surfaces entrain air more readily). | Duez, Ybert, Clanet & Bocquet 2007, Nature Physics 3, 180 |
| Pinch-off | Hydrostatic pressure collapses the cavity walls to a point below the surface (deep seal); thin jets fire up (Worthington jet) and down. The trapped lower cavity rides down with the body as bubbles. | Truscott, Epps & Belden 2014, Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. 46; Aristoff, Truscott, Techet & Bush 2010 |
| Slowing | Drag brakes the body quickly after entry; a near-neutrally-buoyant bottle stops within a short depth and drifts back up. Bubbles overtake it and rise. | basic buoyancy/drag |
| Bubbles | Small bubbles stay round; larger ones (~cm) wobble and flatten as they rise; they look silver from total internal reflection. | standard bubble dynamics |
| Surface from below | Beyond ~49 deg the underside of the surface reflects totally, so it looks like a rippling mirror. | Snell's law, critical angle of water |
