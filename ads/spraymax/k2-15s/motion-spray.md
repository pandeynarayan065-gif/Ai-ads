Real expedition footage near the summit of K2, filmed handheld from behind and slightly to one side of the same mountaineer as the reference image: thick, dark, wind-tossed hair, worn red down suit, harness straps. Close shot at shoulder height. We only ever see the back and side of his head, his ear and his hair, never his face; he keeps looking away toward the horizon and never turns toward the camera.

In the foreground, in sharp focus, he holds the Spraymax bottle from the reference images, exactly as it is: slim white bottle, white ribbed pump collar, white pump head, white label with the vertical indigo "Spraymax" wordmark and the navy ocean-wave band at the base. He grips it low, by the band, in a black liner glove, so the label faces the camera and is never covered; the pump head is turned toward his hair. The bottle stays steady and upright and never changes shape.

He presses the pump twice with his index finger. Each press is a short, sharp burst: the pump head dips, a fine cone of mist shoots from the nozzle into his hair, slows within a hand's width and turns into a soft drifting cloud. The low sun behind him lights the mist up as a glowing haze with tiny bright glints, and the summit wind carries it away past his hair. His hair stays thick and textured as the wind moves it.

Beyond him: a sea of cloud and jagged Karakoram peaks under a dark-blue sky. Natural cold daylight, true colour, no golden tint. Real-time speed, slight handheld movement, no slow motion, no cinematic effects. Its printed label stays identical in every frame. Sound: wind and two quick hisses of the spray, no music, no voices. No faces, no text except the bottle's own label.
